# Chapter 1 architecture

## Purpose

Turn an observed condition into a bounded, answerable research decision.

## Select a reasoning shape

Choose the structure that fits the evidence: broad-to-specific context, documented
problem and consequence, historical development, theoretical contradiction, anomalous
case, or practice gap. Do not force every background into one funnel.

## Required chain

Observed condition → documented evidence → unresolved issue → research question →
objective → benefit → scope and limitation.

Each transition must state why the next element follows. Urgency, prevalence, and
causality require direct evidence.

## Self-check

- The question is not already answered by the background.
- The objective restates the question as a feasible action.
- Benefits name a decision or knowledge contribution rather than “adding insight.”
- Limitations constrain the interpretation, not only the schedule.
